Bluetooth Headphones Not Connecting? Try These Fixes

Published October 2026 · About 4 minutes to read

A calm checklist for pairing problems on phones and laptops. This guide explains what to check first, what to avoid, and when to ask for help.

1. Charge both devices

Low battery can interrupt pairing. Charge the headphones and make sure the phone or computer has Bluetooth enabled.

2. Disconnect from another device

Some headphones reconnect automatically to a tablet or laptop nearby. Turn Bluetooth off temporarily on the other device or disconnect the headphones there.

3. Restart and try again

Restart the phone or computer and power-cycle the headphones. Put the headphones into pairing mode according to their manual.

4. Forget and re-pair

In Bluetooth settings, select the saved headphone entry and choose Forget or Remove. Then scan again and pair from scratch. Menu names vary by device.

5. Check the audio output

If pairing succeeds but you hear nothing, check volume, mute settings and the selected audio output in your music or call app.

6. Check updates and reset guidance

Install official device updates. If the manufacturer offers a headphone reset procedure, follow that model-specific guide rather than guessing button combinations.

7. Test another device

Pair the headphones with a different phone or computer. This helps determine whether the issue follows the headphones or one particular device.

Before you finish

If the headphones become unusually hot, swollen or physically damaged, stop using or charging them and contact the manufacturer.

Settings and features vary by device, software version and region. When in doubt, use the official help pages for your device or service.
