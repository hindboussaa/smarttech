How to Improve Wi-Fi Signal in Every Room

Published October 2026 · About 4 minutes to read

Practical placement and troubleshooting tips for weak home Wi-Fi. This guide explains what to check first, what to avoid, and when to ask for help.

1. Find the weak spots

Run a speed test close to the router and then in the problem room using the same device. If the connection is slow everywhere, the internet service may be the issue rather than Wi-Fi coverage.

2. Move the router into the open

Place it upright where the manufacturer recommends, away from thick walls, large metal objects and enclosed cupboards. A central, elevated location often improves coverage.

3. Try the appropriate band

A 5 GHz or 6 GHz connection can be fast at shorter distances; 2.4 GHz often travels farther but may be more congested. Band names and availability depend on your router.

4. Restart before buying equipment

Restart the router following your provider’s instructions and allow it to reconnect. Check whether a device-specific issue disappears after reconnecting to Wi-Fi.

5. Consider mesh for larger homes

A mesh kit can extend coverage when a single router cannot reach distant rooms. Position mesh nodes where they still receive a good signal, not in the worst dead zone.

6. Use Ethernet for fixed devices

A wired connection for a desktop, TV or games console can be more stable and can reduce competition for wireless capacity.

7. Secure the network

Use WPA2-AES or WPA3 where supported and a strong Wi-Fi password. Update router firmware through the manufacturer or internet provider.

Before you finish

If tests near the router are consistently poor, contact your internet provider with the times and results.

Settings and features vary by device, software version and region. When in doubt, use the official help pages for your device or service.
