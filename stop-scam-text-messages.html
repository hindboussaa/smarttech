How to Spot Scam Text Messages and Fake Delivery Links

Published October 2026 · About 4 minutes to read

Recognise common phishing tricks and protect your accounts. This guide explains what to check first, what to avoid, and when to ask for help.

1. Notice the urgency

Messages claiming your parcel will be returned, your account will close or a fine is due today are designed to rush you. A deadline in a text is not proof that it is genuine.

2. Do not trust the displayed sender

Scammers can imitate familiar names or use lookalike numbers. Even a message appearing in an existing conversation can be suspicious.

3. Check through a separate channel

Instead of tapping the link, open the delivery company’s app or type its known website address yourself. Look up customer support independently.

4. Watch for payment and login traps

A small “redelivery fee” may be a way to collect card details. A fake login page may capture passwords and verification codes. Never enter a one-time code into a page opened from an unexpected text.

5. Report and block

Use your phone’s report-junk or block feature. In the UK, many suspicious texts can be forwarded to 7726; check your mobile provider’s instructions.

6. If you already clicked

If you only opened a page, close it and avoid downloads. If you entered a password, change it through the official site and enable two-step verification. If you gave card details, contact your bank promptly.

Before you finish

When in doubt, stop and verify. Legitimate businesses can be contacted through their official websites or apps.

Settings and features vary by device, software version and region. When in doubt, use the official help pages for your device or service.
