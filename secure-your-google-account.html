How to Secure Your Google Account in 15 Minutes

Published October 2026 · About 4 minutes to read

A straightforward security check-up for Gmail, Drive and other Google services. This guide explains what to check first, what to avoid, and when to ask for help.

1. Start with the official security checkup

Sign in by typing myaccount.google.com into your browser. Open Security and review the Security Checkup suggestions. Check that the displayed account is yours before changing anything. Avoid following account-warning links from unexpected messages.

2. Use a strong, unique password

If you still use a password, make it long and different from every other account. A password manager can generate and store one. If you suspect someone knows your password, change it from the official account page and review recent activity.

3. Add a safer sign-in method

Under Security, review 2-Step Verification and passkeys. A passkey or authenticator is generally safer against phishing than an SMS code. Set up a recovery method you can actually access if your phone is lost.

4. Review signed-in devices

Check Your devices for phones and computers you no longer own or recognise. Sign out of unfamiliar sessions, then change your password if you think someone else accessed your account.

5. Remove unnecessary third-party access

Look at connected apps and services. Remove access for tools you no longer use. Revoking access may stop a connected service working, so confirm you no longer need it.

6. Keep recovery details current

Confirm your recovery email and phone number, where available. Store backup codes securely if you use them. Never share verification codes with anyone who contacts you unexpectedly.

Before you finish

If you see activity you do not recognise, use Google’s official account recovery and security guidance immediately.

Settings and features vary by device, software version and region. When in doubt, use the official help pages for your device or service.
