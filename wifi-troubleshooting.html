What to Do When Your Home Wi-Fi Stops Working

Updated October 2026 · Estimated reading time: 3 minutes

When Wi-Fi fails, first identify whether the problem affects one device or everything connected to your router.

1. Check the scope

Try a second phone or laptop. If only one device fails, its settings may be the cause. If every device fails, focus on the router or broadband service.

2. Check cables and service status

Make sure the router has power and its cables are secure. If possible, check your broadband provider's service-status page using mobile data.

3. Restart the router safely

Unplug its power cable, wait around 30 seconds, reconnect it, and allow several minutes for the connection to return. Avoid pressing the recessed factory-reset button.

4. Reconnect your device

Turn Wi-Fi off and on, then reconnect to your network. If you choose “Forget network,” make sure you know the password first.

5. Improve the signal

Move closer to the router. Thick walls, metal objects, and crowded locations can weaken wireless signals.

6. Contact your provider

If the broadband light still indicates a fault or all devices remain offline, contact your internet provider and explain the checks you already tried.
